# Production Design and Texture

Use this reference to make the frame specific, coherent, and physically believable. Detail must reveal story, place, class, era, weather, or use; it must not become visual clutter.

## Material World Blueprint

Lock these six layers before prompting:

1. **Architecture:** building type, era, geometry, structural material, and social function.
2. **Surface finish:** paint, plaster, tile, timber, concrete, glass, metal, fabric, paper, earth, or vegetation.
3. **Wardrobe:** silhouette, fabric weight, color role, fit, age, weather response, and social context.
4. **Hero prop:** one object that changes the meaning of the action; specify material, scale, wear, and placement.
5. **Atmosphere:** rain, humidity, haze, dust, smoke, snow, heat shimmer, condensation, still air, or clean clarity.
6. **Controlled imperfection:** chipped edge, finger marks, oxidized metal, repaired seam, water stain, faded paint, scuffed floor, wrinkled cotton, or sun bleaching.

## Detail Budget

- Use one hero action, one secondary clue, and no more than three supporting material details.
- Repeat one material or shape to unify the frame; do not repeat every object.
- Give each plane one dominant texture. Avoid equal micro-detail everywhere.
- Keep faces, hands, and the hero prop cleaner in hierarchy than nonessential background texture.
- Make wear directional and causal: rain darkens lower walls, hands polish handles, sunlight fades exposed fabric, traffic dirt gathers near thresholds.

## Precision Vocabulary

Prefer concrete descriptions:

- hand-painted enamel over “vintage”;
- waxed dark timber with worn edges over “rich wood”;
- oxidized brushed steel over “metallic”;
- damp lime plaster with repaired cracks over “old wall”;
- heavy wool with rain-darkened shoulders over “detailed clothing”;
- fluorescent-green reflection on glazed tile over “cinematic lighting.”

## Coherence Gate

Reject the design when:

- architecture, wardrobe, and props imply incompatible eras without narrative reason;
- weather does not affect clothing, ground, glass, or light;
- every surface is pristine or every surface is equally distressed;
- props are present only for decoration;
- material response contradicts the motivated light;
- production design copies a recognizable film location or signature costume.
